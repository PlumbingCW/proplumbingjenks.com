Sewer Gas Smell in the House: Causes and Fixes in Jenks, OK

A rotten-egg or sewage smell means something in your drain system is letting air escape into the house. Most of the time the cause is simple, such as a trap that has dried out, but a strong smell needs attention the same day.

Do these three things first

Open windows and run a fan to move air out. If the smell is strong, you feel unwell, or you cannot find the source, leave the house and call from outside. If the odor smells like natural gas, which is a different, added scent, leave at once and call 911 or your gas utility before you call a plumber.

Sewer gas is a mix of gases produced as waste breaks down. Hydrogen sulfide gives the rotten-egg smell, and methane is also present and can burn. We cannot say what a given smell does to your health, so ask a physician about symptoms. Safety guidelines say hydrogen sulfide can be harmful at higher levels, so ventilate, leave if symptoms occur, and call Poison Help at 1-800-222-1222 with health questions.

Where the smell usually comes from

Your drains are sealed from the sewer by water in a U-shaped trap under every fixture. Anything that breaks that seal or opens a gap lets gas into the room.

Dry P-trap

A sink, tub or shower used rarely loses its trap water to evaporation, and the seal disappears. A guest bathroom is the classic case.

Dry floor drain

Basement and garage floor drains depend on a small water seal. When the water evaporates, gas comes straight up.

Failed wax ring

The wax ring seals the toilet to the drain flange. If the toilet rocks or the ring cracks, odor leaks out around the base.

Blocked or damaged vent

Vent pipes let air into the system so traps hold. A blocked or cracked vent can pull water out of traps and push gas indoors.

Cracked or broken drain line

A crack in a pipe behind a wall, under a slab or in the yard leaks gas and wastewater where you cannot see it.

Sump pit without a lid

An open or poorly sealed sump basin can vent sewer odor into a basement, especially after heavy rain.

Notice where the odor is strongest and when it appears. That narrows the list quickly.

What you can do

What to do: find the source step by step

Walk through the house and smell near each drain. Start with the room where the odor is strongest. Note whether it appears only after a shower, a flush or rain, because timing tells you which part of the system is involved.

Restore trap seals

Run each unused sink, tub and shower for a minute. Pour a quart of water down each floor drain, then add a spoonful of mineral oil to slow evaporation. If the smell fades within an hour, a dry trap was the problem.

Check the toilet and the roof vent

Push the toilet side to side. Any movement means the flange or wax ring has failed, and the toilet needs to be pulled and reset with a new ring. A toilet repair visit also checks the flange for cracks. From the ground, look at the roof vent stack for a bird nest, leaves or ice in winter.

Know when the smell is not yours to fix

If the smell stays after you restore traps and the toilet is solid, the cause is probably a cracked line or a damaged vent. A plumber can run a camera through the line, which is what a sewer camera inspection does, and show you the crack or low spot on video. A confirmed break is repaired through sewer line repair.

Smells in newer subdivisions and small towns

New builds in places like Glenpool and Kiefer often have long runs of PVC and several rarely used bathrooms, so dry traps are the first thing to rule out. Pour water down every drain in the house before you assume a buried problem.

If the smell shows up only during heavy rain, mention that on the phone. Rain-linked odors point toward a sump, a cleanout lid or a vent issue instead of a trap.

When to call right away: stop and phone if

  • ✓The smell is strong enough to cause headaches, nausea or irritated eyes.
  • ✓Sewage or wastewater is backing up in a tub, shower or floor drain.
  • ✓Several drains gurgle at once and the odor fills the house.
  • ✓A damp, stained or sagging area appears on a floor or wall near a drain line.
  • ✓A sump pit or sewer cleanout is overflowing after heavy rain.
  • ✓You smell natural gas, which means leave first and call 911 or the gas utility.
