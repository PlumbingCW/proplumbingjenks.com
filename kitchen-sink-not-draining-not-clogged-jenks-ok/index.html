Kitchen Sink Not Draining but Not Clogged? Jenks, OK Causes

When a kitchen sink will not drain but the pipe under it is clear, the cause is usually the dishwasher drain hose, the disposal outlet, or a shared line downstream.

A simple clog is only in the trap, so plunging or cleaning it fixes it. If the water keeps backing up after that, look at the checks below, because they point to the real source.

  • Dishwasher loop
  • Disposal outlet
  • Shared drain
  • Main line signs

The quick test that separates a clog from something else

Fill one basin with a few inches of water and pull the plug. If it empties slowly, the trap or the disposal is partly blocked. If it empties fine but the other basin fills, the shared tee is blocked. If it empties and then water comes up in a nearby floor drain or tub, the problem is the main or branch line, not the sink.

The usual non-clog culprits

Kitchens carry more hidden connections than a bathroom sink. The dishwasher, the garbage disposal and sometimes a laundry line can all share the same trap arm. When one of them is partly blocked, the sink looks clogged even if the trap is fine. Plumbing codes generally require a trap and a vent on each fixture drain, and according to those standards a vent problem can slow a kitchen sink even when the pipe is clear.

Dishwasher air gap or high loop

A dishwasher drain hose needs either an air gap on the sink deck or a high loop, a hose lifted to the underside of the countertop. Without one, sink water can siphon back or drain slowly. Look under the sink for the corrugated hose and make sure it climbs before it drops to the disposal inlet. If the air gap on the counter is gunky, unscrew the cap and clean the small tube.

Disposal outlet packed with food

Starchy waste like rice, potato peels and pasta can settle in the disposal outlet, the short pipe leaving the unit. The disposal runs and the sink still drains slowly. Unplug the unit, loosen the outlet flange, and clear the gunk out by hand before reassembling. A garbage disposal repair visit also checks the dishwasher knockout plug, which can stay in place after a new install.

Shared drain, tee and baffle

Double bowl sinks tie into one trap arm through a baffle tee. A grease ring or food wad at the baffle blocks one side only. A cable fed from the cleanout beyond the tee reaches it, which a trap cleaning cannot.

Vent and main line problems

A kitchen vent that is blocked makes the drain gurgle and move slowly, and the problem returns whenever the weather changes. A main or branch-line blockage shows up when fixtures lower in the house, like a shower or floor drain, back up first.

Habits that keep a kitchen drain moving

Grease is the quiet enemy of a kitchen line. Wipe pans with a paper towel before washing, and pour cooled fat into a can instead of the drain. Run cold water for ten seconds after the disposal stops, so ground food travels past the trap instead of settling in it.

Avoid feeding the disposal rice, pasta, potato peels, coffee grounds, celery and eggshells, which clump and pack the outlet. A monthly flush with hot water and a squirt of dish soap helps, but it will not cure a packed outlet or a blocked tee. If a drain slows again within a week of cleaning, the buildup is further down the line and a cable is the next step.

Check these before calling

Spend ten minutes on this list. It either solves the problem or gives the plumber a head start.

  • ✓Run the disposal with water on. If it hums or is stuck, turn it off at the switch and reset it before doing anything else.
  • ✓Look at the dishwasher hose. Confirm it rises into a high loop or goes through an air gap, and that it is not kinked.
  • ✓Clean the P-trap. Place a bucket under it, unscrew the slip nuts, and rinse out grease and food.
  • ✓Test the second bowl. Fill both bowls and release them one at a time to find the blocked side.
  • ✓Check other fixtures. If the laundry sink, tub or toilet gurgle or drain slowly, the issue is the main line, so stop and call.
