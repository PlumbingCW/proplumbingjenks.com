Sapulpa overview

Why a 1977 median home year shapes your plumbing

The Census Bureau's 2020 to 2024 survey puts the median Sapulpa house at 1977, and about 55 percent of the 9,817 homes went up before 1980. Emergency dispatch is available around the clock for all of them, and the jobs tend to cluster into a few familiar patterns.

Cast iron drains

Homes of that era often have cast iron waste lines that rust from the inside and scale up, so slow drains return soon after a basic clearing.

Galvanized supply

Older houses may still carry galvanized steel water pipe, which can corrode inside and cause weak flow at the tap or rusty water after a pipe has been disturbed.

Clay sewer laterals

Clay tile yard lines are often found in homes of that vintage. Joints let roots in, and a camera shows whether cleaning is enough.

1970s water heaters

The largest group of local houses dates to the 1970s, and tanks in them get replaced over time. A leaking tank is a same-night call.

Downtown and Route 66

The historic blocks along E Dewey Avenue

Sapulpa's downtown historic district covers about 25 acres, and E Dewey Avenue is part of old Route 66. Census figures show roughly 1,125 homes in the city were built before 1940, and many of them sit in and around that older core.

Houses that old have usually been repiped, relined or patched more than once. A plumber opening a wall may find copper joined to galvanized, or cast iron stubs under the floor, so expect the first visit to be partly about finding out what you have. If a section is failing in several places, repiping often costs less over time than chasing one leak after another.

Rules inside city limits

Licensed and registered contractors only

According to the City of Sapulpa Building Inspection office, plumbing permits are separate from a building permit and must be requested by contractors licensed by the State and registered with the City. The office also says most plumbing work inside city limits must be done by a currently licensed and registered contractor.

For you, that means a handyman swap of a drain or water line may not be allowed. Ask any plumber for proof, and you can verify any plumber's licence with the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board. Permit questions for your address go to the city's Building Inspection office at City Hall Annex.

Before we arrive

If you have a burst pipe or active leak in Sapulpa

Older shutoff valves can seize, so find yours before an emergency. Then work through these four steps while the 24/7 line sends help.

Shut off the water

Turn the main valve clockwise. If it will not move, do not force it with a wrench, and tell dispatch.

Kill power near water

Switch off breakers for wet rooms. Do not step into standing water near outlets.

Clear the area

Move rugs, boxes and furniture. Open drains or catch water with buckets and towels.

City water and sewer

Where the city's responsibility ends

Sapulpa runs its own water treatment, drawing from Sahoma Lake and Skiatook Lake, and has its own wastewater department. The city's report does not publish a hardness figure, so test your tap if scale is a concern.

A rule of thumb: the city handles the main in the street, and a plumber handles the line from the meter into your house. If a clog affects only your home, it is usually yours. If a whole block is down, call the city. Our service area also includes Bixby.

After a storm

Storm damage and the May 2010 tornado

An EF-2 tornado hit northeast Sapulpa on May 13, 2010. After any severe storm, check for bent vent stacks, broken hose bibs and soft ground over the water service line.

A sudden pressure drop after wind damage can mean a broken line outside. Water heater repair is also common after storms, because gas supply trips and relief valves get tested by surges.
