Emergency or planned

Which pages are for emergencies and which are for planning

Pages with Emergency in the title cover problems that should not wait until Monday. Examples are burst pipes, sewer line failures, a flooding water heater and a drain that backs up into the house. The 24/7 line exists for these situations.

Other pages are about planned work, such as tankless water heaters, repiping, new fixture installation and water softeners and filtration. You can call these at any hour too, but nothing is on fire, so use the time to compare options and ask questions.

Some jobs sit in between. A slow drain or a dripping faucet is not an emergency today, though it often becomes one when ignored. If water is spreading, a drain is backing up, or you have no hot water in cold weather, treat it as urgent.

Before you call

What to do first, whatever the service

Find your main water shutoff before you need it. In many houses it is near the meter, in the garage or at the point where the line enters the house. Closing it stops most active leaks within a minute.

Do not mix drain chemicals or force a clog with sharp tools. Both can make a plumber's job harder. For anything involving a gas smell, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before calling us.

Pricing

How pricing works across all services

We do not list prices on service pages because the same label covers very different jobs. A drain cleaning in a first-floor sink is not the same as clearing a main line from a cleanout in the yard.

What we do promise is a clear price before work starts. The plumber assesses the problem, explains the options, and tells you the cost. You decide whether to go ahead. If the job needs a permit, that is a separate step, since according to Jenks Building Inspections the city adopted the 2018 IPC and Oklahoma law exempts leak repairs from permits.

Commercial

Businesses and larger properties

Restaurants, offices and small retail spaces have different plumbing needs than a home. Grease, high-use restrooms and shared lines change the repair. The commercial plumbing page explains how those calls are handled.
