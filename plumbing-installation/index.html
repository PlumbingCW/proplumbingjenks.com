Code and permits

Permits and the plumbing code in Jenks

The City of Jenks adopted the 2018 International Plumbing Code (IPC), effective April 2022. Oklahoma state law (Title 59, section 1015) requires a plumbing permit for work, with an exception for leak repairs. Permits in the city go through Jenks Building Inspections at 918-556-7439.

That matters for a new shower valve, a relocated sink or a new gas branch, because inspectors look at venting, trap arms, pipe support and test results. Oklahoma lets an individual do plumbing on their own residence, but you can verify any plumber's licence with the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board if you hire out the work.

Swapping a fixture versus running new lines

The job type changes the scope, the permit question and the time on site. For a new toilet or faucet, look for the EPA WaterSense label, and keep the manufacturer installation instructions handy for the inspector.

Fixture swap in the same spot

  • Reuses existing supply and drain locations
  • Often done in one visit
  • Main risk is a corroded stop valve or stripped fitting
  • A permit question for the plumber to settle
  • Cost driven by the fixture and parts

New or relocated lines

  • Needs new supply, drain and vent runs
  • Walls, floor or slab may be opened
  • Permit and inspection are part of the schedule
  • Pipe size and slope must meet code
  • Cost driven by access and run length

New construction, backflow and gas: jobs with extra rules

New construction plumbing

Jenks keeps growing. Census ACS 2020-2024 data shows about 3,300 homes built from 2010 to 2019 and about 500 since 2020. If you are building or finishing a basement or addition, plumbing happens in stages: rough-in before walls close, then fixture set and final inspection. Ask your builder who owns each stage.

Backflow testing

A backflow preventer is a valve assembly that keeps contaminated water from flowing back into drinking water, and it is common on irrigation systems and some commercial lines. Whether your property needs one, and who may test it, depends on the water provider. Confirm the requirement with the City of Jenks before you buy or test anything, because we do not state program rules we have not verified.

Gas line inspection and installation

If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first, then call a plumber. For planned work, such as a new range, dryer or grill line, the plumber checks pipe material, shutoff valves and fittings, and pressure tests the line. Scope is confirmed on the call, since some gas work involves other trades and permits.

Low water pressure: pressure regulators and supply sizing

Weak flow at every faucet often starts at the pressure regulator, a bell-shaped valve near where the main enters the house. A plumber threads a gauge onto a hose bib to read static pressure. Many codes cap static pressure around 80 psi, and a worn regulator can leave it too low or too high.

If only one bathroom is weak, look at supply sizing. A long run of half-inch pipe feeding a rain showerhead and a tub filler cannot deliver enough flow, and the fix is a larger branch or a manifold. Clogged aerators and a half-closed valve are quick checks first.

If neighbors have the same problem, the issue may be on the city side. The Jenks Water Department publishes 918-299-5363 for water service questions.
