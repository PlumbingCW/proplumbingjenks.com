City rules

Permits, water and the new sewer plant

The City of Glenpool buys all its water from Tulsa's A.B. Jewell plant and provides sewer and trash service. Tulsa's FAQ puts hardness at about 8.2 grains per gallon, the same source Jenks and Bixby use, so scale on a tank bottom is a normal long-term concern.

Water heater replacement needs a permit

The Glenpool Building Department's Required Residential Permits list names water heater replacements, along with moving fixtures, altering vents and changing gas, sewer or water lines. A trade permit requires current contractor registration. Ask any plumber, ours included, how the permit will be handled before work starts.

A lagoon system and a new plant

The City of Glenpool says its sewer is still lagoon-style while a new wastewater plant is under construction under a state DEQ consent order. Commissioning is expected in fall 2026. Work on the city side does not change who owns your lateral, so a backup inside your home is still yours to fix, and sewer line repair starts at your cleanout.

Why the oil-town past matters

The Glenn Pool oil strike of November 1905 brought the first building boom, and the Oklahoma Historical Society notes more booms in the 1950s and 1970s. If your house sits near the old center or dates to the 1970s, ask for a camera look at the drain line before assuming newer pipe.

Before we arrive

If you have a burst pipe or active leak in Glenpool

Do these while the 24/7 line sends help. They take a few minutes and can save a floor.

Close the main

Turn the main shutoff clockwise at the house. If water still runs, it may be before the valve, so tell dispatch.

Kill power to wet areas

Switch off breakers for any wet room. Stay out of standing water near outlets and appliances.

Clear the work area

Move rugs and boxes, and open the cabinet under the sink or the closet around the tank.
