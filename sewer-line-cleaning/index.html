Sewer line cleaning in Jenks, Oklahoma means clearing the main line that carries all the house's drainage toward the public sewer in Tulsa County. A sewer backup is a main line problem until proven otherwise

Every fixture in the house drains into one pipe, and a blockage in that pipe has nowhere to push waste except up. The lowest opening gives way first, which is usually a basement or ground-level floor drain, a tub or a shower.

If only the kitchen sink is slow, the clog is local. If a toilet gurgles when the washer drains, or two bathrooms back up together, the blockage is on the main line.

The City of Jenks Public Works department maintains the city's sewer system. If neighbors are also backing up or you see sewage in the street, call Public Works as well, because the problem may be in the public main.

What causes a main line to clog

Most main line clogs trace to a short list of causes.

  • ✓Tree roots. Fine roots enter at joints and trap paper until the line fills, which is why backups can recur on a schedule.
  • ✓Grease buildup. Kitchen fat cools in the horizontal run and narrows the opening year by year.
  • ✓Wipes and hygiene products. Labeled flushable wipes do not break up like paper and snag on any rough joint.
  • ✓Bellied or sagging pipe. A low spot holds water and solids that eventually settle into a plug.
  • ✓Foreign objects. Toys, rags and kitchen debris wash in through a floor drain or a broken fixture.

The work

How the main line gets cleared

The plumber starts at the cleanout, the capped pipe that gives direct access to the main line without removing a toilet. It is usually outside near the house or in a basement, and a cleanout is the first thing to point out when you call. Plumbing codes generally call for cleanouts at set points on drain lines, and Jenks has adopted the 2018 International Plumbing Code (IPC), so ask the plumber to add one if yours is missing.

Cable first

A heavy cable with a cutting head is fed down the line until it reaches the blockage. For roots, a blade shaves them back inside the pipe. For a grease or paper plug, a spearhead tears through it and the cable pulls debris out.

Jetting when the wall is coated

If the cable opens a path but the pipe wall is lined with grease or scale, high-pressure jetting washes it clean. The method and its limits are on our hydro jetting page.

Camera after the line is open

With flow restored, a camera shows what caused the blockage and whether the pipe is sound. A recording of roots entering a joint or a bellied section tells you whether you need a repair or simply regular cleaning. See sewer camera inspection for what the camera shows.

Repeat clogs and what to do about them

A main line that clogs twice in a year has a cause that cleaning alone will not remove.

Roots

Cleaning buys months, not years. Spot repair or lining closes the entry point.

Grease

Change what goes down the kitchen drain, and schedule jetting if buildup returns.

A belly

Water pooling in a low section cannot be cleaned away. It needs a dig and regrade.

Offset joint

Pieces that no longer line up catch debris. A camera finds it and a repair fixes it.

Cracked pipe

Soil and roots enter the break. Repair is the answer, and jetting through it makes it worse.

What to do if sewage is coming up now

These steps limit damage and keep people safe while you wait for a plumber, day or night.

Stop all water

No flushing, no laundry, no dishwasher, no showers. Water used upstairs ends up in the lowest drain.

Keep clear of the water

Sewage carries bacteria. Keep children and pets away, and wear gloves and boots if you must handle anything.

Clear the way to the cleanout

Leave the cap on unless the plumber asks, since backed-up waste can spill out. Just clear a path to it.

Lift belongings

Move rugs, boxes and electronics off the floor. Turn off power to any wet area if water is near outlets.
