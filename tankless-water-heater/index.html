Hot water runs out or turns lukewarm?

If your hot water fades to lukewarm mid-shower, swings between hot and cold, or never gets hot enough, a tankless unit is usually blocked, starved or mis-set rather than broken. The usual causes are scale on the heat exchanger, a clogged inlet filter, too many fixtures running at once or a failing sensor.

The fix is called tankless descaling and flushing: a plumber circulates a descaling solution through the heat exchanger, rinses it, then cleans the filter and checks the controls. Scale is why this matters in Jenks, and the section below explains it.

Tankless flush and descale: what the visit involves

Most units have isolation valves that make the flush simple. The plumber follows the manufacturer's descaling instructions for your model. Installing or replacing a unit falls under the 2018 International Plumbing Code (IPC), International Fuel Gas Code (IFGC) and International Mechanical Code (IMC) that Jenks adopted, so ask Jenks Building Inspections about a permit.

1

Read the code and the history

The plumber checks the display for error codes, the flow and temperature settings and the age and model of the unit.

2

Isolate the heater

Power or gas goes off, the cold and hot isolation valves close, and a small pump and hoses connect to the service ports.

3

Circulate descaling solution

A food-safe descaler (many manufacturers call for white vinegar) runs through the heat exchanger for the time the instructions call for. This dissolves scale that restricts flow and makes the unit overheat.

4

Flush and clean the filter

Fresh water rinses the solution out, and the cold water inlet screen is pulled and cleaned of grit.

5

Test the unit

The valves reopen, air is purged, and the plumber fires the unit and checks temperature rise, flow and any gas or vent connections.

How tankless works

What a tankless water heater is and where it struggles

A tankless water heater is a wall-hung unit that heats water as it passes through a heat exchanger, using a gas burner or electric elements. There is no stored tank, so it never runs out of stored hot water, but it has a flow limit: a gas unit can only raise so many gallons per minute by a set number of degrees.

That limit is why two showers and a dishwasher can overwhelm a unit sized for one bath. Gas models also need a larger gas line and a sealed stainless or plastic vent. Safety comes first with any gas unit, and gas line questions are confirmed on the call.

Hard water is the other issue. The City of Tulsa publishes about 8.2 grains per gallon of hardness for its A.B. Jewell plant, and Jenks buys its water from Tulsa. That does not tell you the exact figure at your tap, which a test kit or a call to the city can confirm.

Even moderately hard water leaves scale in a heat exchanger over time, so a regular flush protects the unit. If you want to reduce scale at the source, see water softener and filtration.
