Commercial plumbing service for Jenks buildings

Commercial plumbing is repair, drain service and fixture work on the water, drain, waste and vent systems of buildings that serve customers or employees. The pipes are larger, restrooms see more traffic, and one failure can close a whole suite.

A licensed plumber handles the calls that stop a business: hidden leaks, clogged mainlines, floor drain overflow, lift pump failures and stopped-up restrooms. Grease trap pumping is something to confirm on the call. Jenks has adopted the 2018 International Plumbing Code (IPC), and Oklahoma law requires a plumbing permit for work other than leak repairs, so ask Jenks Building Inspections before a restroom change.

Call (855) 575-2890 and describe the building, the symptom and who can reach the shutoff. That short conversation decides the first move.

Water pooling in a commercial building: when the puddle is not the source

Water rarely stays where the pipe failed.

  • ✓Damp base of a shared wall. Water follows studs and slab edges, so the wet spot may sit a full unit away from the failed pipe.
  • ✓Stained or sagging ceiling tiles. Check above the stain for a supply line, a drain joint or an air handler condensate line, and ask the suite overhead.
  • ✓Meter moving after close. With every fixture off, watch the low-flow dial on the water meter. Any movement means water is going somewhere.

The methods for tracing the source are covered on the leak detection page.

What to do before the plumber arrives at your business

Locate the shutoff

Find the suite valve and the building main. If the main sits in a locked room, get the key holder on the phone.

Protect people and stock

Lift product off the floor and close off areas with standing water, especially near outlets.

Document the damage

Take wide and close photos with timestamps before anything is moved. Insurers and landlords both ask for them.

Reach the right contact

Call the owner or manager and arrange building access, alarm codes and roof or ceiling entry.

Tenant, landlord and shared walls: who calls the plumber

Lease terms decide who pays. The line serving only your space is often the tenant's concern, while risers, mains and shared wall lines are often the owner's.

Water coming through from the next suite

The source may be a neighbor's fixture or a common line. Tracing and pressure testing settle it with facts.

After-hours shutoff plans

Every tenant should know where the suite valve and the building main are, and who holds the key to the mechanical room. For retail, put the shutoff location on the closing checklist.

Drains, floor drains, lift pumps and grease traps

Commercial drain service starts with a cable machine for stoppages and a camera for repeat clogs. When grease or scale coats the pipe wall, hydro jetting scours it where a cable only punches a hole.

Floor drain overflow

A floor drain backs up when the line downstream is blocked, and smells when its trap seal dries out. Repeated overflow points to the branch or mainline, which is a drain cleaning job. Plumbing codes generally call for cleanouts at set points on drain lines, so ask the plumber to show you where yours are.

Lift pumps and sump pits

Restrooms below the sewer line often drain into a pit with a lift pump. A stuck float, failed check valve or dead motor lets the pit fill.

Grease trap pumping

Kitchens use a grease trap or interceptor to catch fats before they reach the sewer. Whether we can pump a given trap is something to confirm on the call. Ask the City of Jenks which kitchen rules apply to your business.
