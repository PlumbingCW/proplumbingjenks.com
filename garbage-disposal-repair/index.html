Right now

Disposal jammed, leaking or dead right now

Start with power. Turn the wall switch off and then switch off the breaker, because a switch can be bumped back on. Never put your hand in the opening. Even a stopped unit can start, and the blades are sharp.

If it hums but the blades do not turn, the motor is jammed. If it is silent, try the red reset button on the bottom. If water drips from underneath, find where it comes from before you do anything else.

What goes wrong with a disposal

Each symptom points to a different part.

Hum with no spin

Something is wedged between the flywheel and the shredder ring. A hex key in the bottom socket turns the flywheel back and forth to free it.

Silent, no hum

The overload has tripped or power is off. Wait a few minutes, then press the reset button on the bottom.

Leak at the top flange

The seal under the sink flange has dried or loosened. It drips when the sink is used.

Leak at the body

A crack in the housing or a failed internal seal. This usually means the unit is done.

Leak at the drain outlet

The discharge elbow gasket has loosened or the pipe nut is not tight.

Smell and slow drain

Food film clings to the grinding chamber and baffle. Heavy clogs sit beyond the disposal in the trap or branch drain.

Repair or replace the disposal

Age, brand and where the problem sits decide it.

Repair makes sense when

  • The jam clears with a hex key
  • The reset button brings it back to life
  • A flange or elbow gasket is the only leak
  • The unit is fairly new
  • The drain line is the real clog

Replace when

  • The housing leaks
  • The motor stays dead after reset
  • The unit is old and repeatedly jams
  • Rust or corrosion shows underneath
  • You want a stronger or quieter model
Choosing and living with one

Horsepower, what to feed it and the dishwasher connection

A disposal is only as good as the match to your kitchen. The 2018 International Plumbing Code (IPC), adopted by Jenks, expects a disposal to drain through a trap, and the manufacturer installation instructions cover the dishwasher knockout, so follow both when swapping a unit.

Horsepower in plain words

A one-third horsepower unit handles light use. Half horsepower is a common middle choice, three-quarters grinds tougher scraps with less jamming, and one horsepower suits a busy kitchen. Builder-grade disposals in newer Jenks homes are often at the smaller end, so a family that cooks a lot may outgrow it.

What goes down it

Small soft scraps are fine. Grease, coffee grounds in bulk, rice, pasta, potato peels, fibrous stalks and bones cause clogs or jams. Run cold water during and after use. Grease cools and coats the drain line even if the disposal handles it.

Dishwasher drain connection

Most dishwashers drain through the disposal. A new unit has a knockout plug in the dishwasher inlet that must be punched out, or the dishwasher will not drain. The hose should loop high under the counter. If the dishwasher backs up into the sink, check this connection first.

Smell fixes

Ice and citrus peels freshen the chamber, but they do not clean the baffle. Scrub the underside of the rubber splash guard with a brush.

What to do before the plumber arrives

These steps are safe to do yourself.

Cut the power

Wall switch off, then the breaker. Never reach into the opening.

Empty the cabinet

Clear the space under the sink so the plumber has room and nothing gets wet.

Try the hex key

Insert a quarter-inch hex key in the bottom socket and work it back and forth. Press reset after.

Skip the chemicals

Drain cleaners in a stuck disposal damage seals and splash skin.

Why the breaker matters

A wall switch can be flipped back on by anyone in the house, and the unit restarts. Turning off the breaker is the only way to be sure the motor is dead. Use a tool, such as tongs, to remove anything stuck, never your fingers.
