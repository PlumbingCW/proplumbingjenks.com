The short answer

When a burst pipe cannot wait

A burst pipe is any supply or drain line that has split, cracked or blown a fitting open and is leaking more than a drip. If you can see water spraying, pooling or coming through a ceiling, close the main valve at once. The repair itself is a cut-and-replace job: the damaged length is removed, a new section is joined in with proper fittings, and the line is pressure tested.

Not every wet spot is a burst. A pinhole in copper or a loose joint is a smaller leak, and a hidden loss behind a wall may need leak detection before anyone cuts. A split with water actively moving is the one that needs a plumber tonight.

How a burst pipe shows itself

These are the signs that the pipe has failed, not just a fixture.

  • ✓Water you can see. A spray, a steady stream or a puddle spreading across a floor from a wall, ceiling or cabinet.
  • ✓No water at the taps. Pressure drops to nothing or a trickle because the supply is escaping elsewhere, or a main valve has failed.
  • ✓Noise in the walls. Hissing, banging or running-water sounds with every fixture closed.
  • ✓A ceiling that sags or bubbles. Water is pooling above the drywall, and the drywall may be close to dropping.
  • ✓Brown or cloudy water. Sudden discoloration after a freeze or pressure surge can mean a pipe has shifted or cracked.

What a repair visit involves

Most burst pipe visits follow the same order.

1

Stop the water and assess

The plumber confirms the main is off, drains the line and finds the failed section. Wet drywall or flooring is checked so you know how much to open.

2

Cut out the damaged pipe

A pipe cutter removes the split length with a few inches of margin on each side, so the new joint sits on sound pipe.

3

Join in new pipe

Copper is soldered or joined with press fittings, PEX uses crimp or expansion fittings, and CPVC uses solvent cement. Drain pipe is replaced with matching PVC or cast iron couplings.

4

Pressure test

The line is brought back to normal pressure and watched for any drop. A test that holds lets the plumber close the wall.

5

Walk through the damage

You get a straight list of what dried, what must be replaced and what to tell your insurer.

Shut it off before the plumber arrives

These steps limit damage and are safe for most homeowners.

Find and close the main

The main shut-off is usually where the water line enters the house, in a utility area, garage or near the water heater. Turn it clockwise until it stops. If it will not turn, do not force it, and tell the dispatcher.

Open a low faucet

With the main closed, open the lowest tap in the house to drain the lines and stop residual flow.

Kill power near the water

Switch off the breaker for any room where water touches outlets, fixtures or appliances. If you must wade through water to reach the panel, call the 24/7 line instead.

Thaw only with safe heat

For a frozen pipe, use a hair dryer on low or warm towels from the faucet toward the frozen part. Never use an open flame, torch or heat gun, because pipes, insulation and framing can ignite.

Winter in Jenks

Freeze and thaw: why pipes split in this region

NWS Tulsa 1991-2020 normals put the January average low at 28.0 F, with about 72 days a year at or below 32 F. That matters because water expands as it freezes, and the split usually shows after the thaw, when pressure returns. Pipes along exterior walls, in garages and in unheated crawl spaces are the first to fail.

Cold also hits public lines. The Jenks freeze-thaw main break on January 8, 2014 cut water to more than half the city for hours, according to news coverage. In a cold snap, drip a tap, open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls and know where your main valve is before you need it.

Copper pipe repair

Copper splits along the seam when ice forms, and it also develops pinholes from corrosion. A short split is cut out and sleeved with new copper using solder or a press fitting. If the copper has pitted along a long run, replacing the run is more honest than patching every pinhole.

Cracked drain pipes

A cracked drain line shows as a wet floor under a fixture, a smell or a slow drain. The cracked length is cut out and replaced with matching pipe. A sewer-side crack is a different job that starts with a camera, covered under sewer line repair.

Pipe replacement instead of repair

When a pipe has failed twice, the better call is replacing the section or the line. If whole-house plumbing is the issue, the repiping page covers what that involves.

Temporary clamp or full section replacement

Some repairs are a fix for tonight and some are meant to last.

Clamp or sleeve

  • Fast way to stop a small split or pinhole
  • Useful to stop damage until parts arrive
  • Leaves the original pipe in place
  • Not ideal for a long cracked section
  • May be a temporary step only

Cut-out and replace

  • Removes the damaged length completely
  • Restores normal pressure rating
  • Takes longer and opens more wall
  • Better for repeated or long failures
  • Matches the pipe to what is already there
