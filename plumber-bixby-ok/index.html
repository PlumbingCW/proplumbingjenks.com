Spring and storm season

When the Arkansas River sets the plumbing agenda

The Arkansas River divides Bixby into north and south parts. On May 22, 2019, the City of Bixby recommended evacuation of floodplain areas ahead of increased Keystone Dam releases, and local news warned of one to two feet of water in several neighborhoods and downtown.

The plumbing lesson holds either way. Outside floodwater can push back through a sewer lateral and surface at the lowest drain in the house, and it can soak a water heater or furnace that sits on a garage or utility room floor. Floodwater in the yard is not a plumbing leak, but a backup inside the house is the plumber's problem.

Sewer backups during high water

If a floor drain or tub bubbles during heavy rain, stop running sinks, showers and the washer. Every gallon you add has nowhere to go but out. A licensed plumber can clear the line with sewer line cleaning once the water recedes, and a cleanout with a backwater valve is worth asking about.

Water heaters that sat in water

A tank whose burner or element was submerged should not be relit by a homeowner, because gas controls and elements can fail after wetting. A water heater repair visit tests the controls first, and replacement is the honest answer if corrosion is inside.

Winter

Freeze season: what the Tulsa normals mean for your pipes

The National Weather Service's 1991-2020 normals for the Tulsa station put the January average low at 28 degrees, with about 72 days a year at or below freezing. The average last freeze is around March 29 and the first falls near November 3. That is a long window for exposed hose bibs, garage supply lines and attic runs.

In February 2021, Tulsa hit minus 3 degrees and the City of Tulsa logged 450 water line breaks in under a month. Bixby buys Tulsa water. Before a hard freeze, disconnect hoses, open cabinet doors on exterior walls and let a faucet drip. If a pipe bursts, close the main and call the line for burst pipe repair.

Summer

Lawn season and the leak you cannot see

Heavy summer watering means a lot of pipe under the grass. A broken sprinkler line or a stuck valve can waste water for weeks before the bill shows it. Turn off every tap and appliance, then read the meter. If the dial still moves, there is a leak somewhere between the meter and the house.

Bixby Public Works Authority sells water it buys from the City of Tulsa, and the city lists main-line pressure at 110 PSI. A pressure regulator brings that down inside the house, and a failing one shows as thumping pipes or a weeping relief valve. Ask the plumber to test pressure at a hose bib, and use leak detection when the meter keeps moving.
