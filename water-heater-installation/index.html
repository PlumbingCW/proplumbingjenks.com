Planning

What a water heater installation includes

Water heater installation means placing a new unit where none exists, or moving or upgrading one, and connecting its water, fuel, venting and safety parts correctly. The work covers the shutoff valve, flexible connectors, a temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge pipe, a drain pan where required, and a vent for gas models. It sits inside the wider category of plumbing installation, which also covers fixtures and new supply lines.

Census ACS data puts the median Jenks home at 2006 construction and shows roughly 3,300 homes built from 2010 to 2019. That means a lot of newer houses with builder-grade heaters now coming due, and many owners use that moment to resize or relocate rather than copy what the builder put in.

Safety and code

Gas, venting and permit questions

Planned work gives you time to check the details that matter most for safety and for passing inspection.

Gas connection and gas line work

A gas heater needs a correctly sized line, a shutoff valve, a sediment trap or drip leg where required, and a leak test at every joint. Safety comes first with gas, so if you smell it at any time, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before anyone else. Whether new gas line work is needed, and what it involves, is confirmed on the call and again on site.

Venting and combustion air

Gas heaters must carry exhaust outside through a vent with proper slope and clearance, and they need enough air to burn cleanly. Condensing and power-vent models use plastic pipe and can run horizontally. Electric models need none of this.

Relief valve, expansion tank and pan

Every tank needs a temperature and pressure relief valve piped to a safe drain point. An expansion tank is usually added when the home has a check valve or pressure regulator, and a pan with a drain protects floors in tight or upper spaces.

Permits

Do not assume a rule either way. The City of Jenks Building Inspections office publishes its line at 918-556-7439, so you can ask the city building department what applies to your project. The plumber on the job handles permit questions as part of the work.

Types of installs this page covers

New construction

Rough-in of supply, gas, vent and drain lines before the walls close, then the heater set at trim-out.

Relocating the heater

Moving it from a closet to the garage, or the reverse, with new lines and venting.

Upgrading size or efficiency

Stepping up to a bigger tank, a high-efficiency model or a heat pump water heater.

Tank to tankless

A wall-hung unit needs a larger gas line, a stainless or plastic vent and a power outlet.

Remodels and additions

Adding a bathroom or laundry may need a second heater or a recirculation line.
