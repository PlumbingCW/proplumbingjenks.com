Emergency Hydro Jetting in Jenks, OK

If the same line has backed up again after a cable run, do not keep paying for the same short fix. Our line is open 24/7, so stop using water on that line and call for a plumber who can look at what is lining the pipe.

Hydro jetting in Jenks, Oklahoma is cleaning a drain or sewer line with a high-pressure water hose and a nozzle that scours the inside of the pipe. It is the step up from drain cleaning with a cable, used in Tulsa County homes when grease, scale, silt or roots keep rebuilding the blockage.

Drains that keep clogging after snaking

A cable punches a hole through a blockage and pulls some of it out. The grease and scale stuck to the pipe wall stay behind, so the hole narrows again and the drain slows within weeks.

Hydro jetting washes that layer off instead of piercing it. A nozzle with rear-facing jets pulls itself down the pipe while forward and side jets cut the buildup loose and flush it downstream.

How a jetting visit runs

The order matters, because jetting sends a lot of water at high pressure into the line.

1

Locate access and confirm the pipe

The plumber finds a cleanout or pulls a fixture to get into the line, and asks what pipe material you have and how old the section is.

2

Camera first when in doubt

A small camera on a cable shows the grease layer, root mass, offset joint or crack before any pressure goes in.

3

Thread the jet hose

The hose and nozzle are fed in and the pressure is raised slowly, matched to the pipe size and material.

4

Work the line in passes

The nozzle is drawn back and forth through the problem stretch until the wall is clean and the debris has flushed to the main.

5

Camera again and a flow test

A second look shows what is left, then water is run through the fixtures to confirm the line drains freely.

Honest limits

When jetting is the wrong call

High-pressure water will find any weak spot. A collapsed section, a crack or a badly offset joint can be made worse, and soil can wash into the gap. Orangeburg, a tar-paper pipe used in some mid-century lines, and very worn clay can be too fragile for the pressure.

Census data shows about 16% of Jenks homes were built before 1980, so most lines here are newer plastic, but the older pockets near downtown may have clay or cast iron. That is the reason a camera comes first when the pipe material is unknown. If the camera shows damage, the plumber will say so and quote a repair instead of jetting through it.

Roots are the other caveat. Jetting can clear them out of a joint, but they come back until the crack that let them in is sealed. See sewer camera inspection for how the camera finds the entry point. Plumbing codes generally call for cleanouts on drain lines, and Jenks adopted the 2018 International Plumbing Code (IPC), so locate your cleanout before the visit because a jetter usually enters there.

What to do when a line is backing up and cannot wait

A backed-up line is urgent. These steps keep the damage small until a plumber can come, including after hours.

Stop sending water down

Hold off on showers, the washer and flushes. Water has nowhere to go and will rise at the lowest drain.

Find the cleanout

Look for a capped pipe near the house or in the yard, and clear the area so the plumber can reach it.

Note the history

Write down when it last clogged and whether it was cabled. That tells the plumber whether jetting is the sensible next step.

Move belongings

Lift items off the floor in a basement, laundry room or garage in case the line backs up before the visit.

When a repeat clog points to jetting

If more than one of these fits your home, ask for jetting on the call.

  • ✓Back to slow within a month. A cable cleared it, and the drain slid back to slow quickly.
  • ✓Greasy kitchen line. Kitchen waste goes to a long horizontal run that holds cooled fat.
  • ✓Multiple fixtures slow together. The restriction is on the shared line, not one fixture.
  • ✓Smell with no visible leak. Debris coating the wall holds odor even after the line drains.
  • ✓Roots found on camera. A previous inspection showed fine roots and no broken pipe.
